# Responsive Web Visualization 1

## Context: When

The first web visualization addresses the temporal question of the project:

> **How does the composition of violence change over time?**

The objective is not only to identify when the conflict becomes more or less intense, but to determine **how its form changes from week to week**. For this reason, the data is aggregated at a **weekly level** rather than shown as individual events.

Each week becomes a comparable temporal unit containing counts for state-based, non-state, and one-sided violence, together with the corresponding one-sided share and civilian fatalities.

---

## Representation: Timeline

Visually, the representation is a **multi-panel temporal visualization** composed of three horizontally aligned charts.

![web 1 Absolute](https://github.com/PayThePizzo/war-civilian-violence/blob/main/docs/phase_3/images/Web1.png?raw=TRUE)

The top panel occupies most of the vertical space and represents the weekly composition of conflict events. The middle panel isolates the proportion of events classified as one-sided violence, while the bottom panel emphasizes civilian fatalities from one-sided violence using discrete lollipop marks. 

Because the panels share the same horizontal scale, a week can be compared vertically across all three measures.

### What it shows

Three time-aligned panels sharing one x-axis (week):

1. **Event composition** - stacked area of `state_based_event_count` / `non_state_event_count` /
   `one_sided_event_count`, in either absolute or composition-percent mode.
2. **One-sided share** - `one_sided_event_share` as a thin area/line.
3. **One-sided civilian fatalities** - `one_sided_civilian_fatalities` as lollipops (stem + dot),
   only drawn for weeks with a non-zero value.

Below the chart, we also find a **drag-to-zoom brush strip** (to select a subset period) and a "View as table" fallback.

The top panel can be switched between two complementary representations:

- **Absolute events** shows the actual weekly number of state-based, non-state, and one-sided events. This mode is useful for identifying increases and decreases in the overall intensity of recorded conflict activity.
- **Composition %** normalizes each week's events to 100%. This removes the effect of the total number of events and instead emphasizes the changing relative importance of the three categories.

![web 1 Composition](https://github.com/PayThePizzo/war-civilian-violence/blob/main/docs/phase_3/images/Web1%20-%20Comp.png?raw=TRUE)

The two modes answer slightly different questions. Absolute mode asks **"how much violence occurred?"**, while composition mode asks **"what kind of violence made up that week's activity?"**.

The second panel provides a more direct reading of this shift by plotting `one_sided_event_share`.

- A rise in this line indicates that one-sided violence accounts for a larger fraction of the week's recorded events, regardless of whether the total number of events is high or low.

The third panel separates **frequency from human impact**. 

- A week may contain only a limited number of one-sided events but still produce an exceptionally high number of civilian fatalities.

The visualization is also interactive:

- **Actor scope:** re-renders whenever `selectedActorId` changes (global);
- **Hover:** a shared crosshair line across all three panels;
  numbers. Local state only.
- **Click a week:** writes `focusWeek` to the global store - this is what drives the Map into Week
  mode and highlights T0 elsewhere.
- **Drag-to-zoom:** the brush strip below the axis rescales the x-domain of all three panels
  (clamped to the actor's full date range). Entirely local - never touches the shared store, and
  resets available via a "Reset zoom" button that appears only while zoomed.
- **Keyboard:** arrow keys move a virtual cursor over the chart, Enter/Space sets `focusWeek`,
  matching the mouse-click behavior.

## Pros and Cons

| **Pro** | **Con** |
|---------|---------|
| The three synchronized panels make it possible to compare **event frequency, composition and civilian consequences** at exactly the same point in time. | Three panels and several metrics make the visualization more complex than a conventional single-series timeline. |
| Switching between **absolute values and percentages** separates overall conflict intensity from changes in violence composition. | Percentage mode can visually exaggerate the importance of a category during weeks with very few total events. |
| Temporal trends, spikes and major discontinuities are immediately visible. | Very large fatality outliers compress the scale of the lower panel and can make smaller but still meaningful episodes visually difficult to distinguish. |
| Hover, zoom, actor filtering and linked week selection support detailed exploration without losing the conflict-wide temporal context. | Interactive features such as tooltips and zoom contain information that cannot be communicated completely in a static screenshot. |

## Knowledge: What can we learn from it?

The visualization allows us to examine whether weeks dominated by direct armed confrontation are followed, accompanied, or replaced by periods in which one-sided violence accounts for a greater proportion of recorded events.

The distinction between the three panels is important because **event counts and consequences are not equivalent**. This is difficult to understand from `weekly_metrics.csv` alone.

The timeline converts these values into **position, shape, continuity and aligned temporal patterns**. Instead of reading and comparing hundreds of rows manually, the user can perceive periods, peaks, transitions and co-movements directly.

The absolute and composition modes are particularly useful together. The absolute view preserves the magnitude of conflict activity, while the composition view reveals structural change.

The lower fatality panel adds another important piece of knowledge: **the severity of one-sided violence is not necessarily proportional to its frequency**. for instance, El Fasher massacre during October 2025 illustrates this clearly. Its exceptional fatality spike would be difficult to understand by looking only at event counts or percentages.

## Pipeline from raw information to the visualizations

The timeline reads one derived file, `weekly_metrics.csv`. All analytical work happens upstream in the Python pipeline, while the browser only filters, scales and lays out. 

The whole pipeline runs with `python scripts/build_all.py`.

**What is inside the raw dataset.**

- The UCDP Georeferenced Event Dataset (GED, v26.1) has one row per event: date range (`date_start`, `date_end`), `type_of_violence` (1 state-based, 2 non-state, 3 one-sided), the two sides, fatality estimates (`deaths_a`, `deaths_b`, `deaths_civilians`, `best`/`low`/`high`) and coordinates. The project keeps Sudan events dated 2023-04-15 to 2025-12-31.

**What is inside `weekly_metrics.csv`.** 

- One row per actor × Monday-starting week, including a synthetic conflict-wide actor (`actor_id = "__ALL__"`).

The timeline plots these columns:

| Column | Used for |
|---|---|
| `week_start` | shared x axis |
| `state_based_event_count`, `non_state_event_count`, `one_sided_event_count` | panel 1, stacked area (absolute or percent mode) |
| `one_sided_event_share` | panel 2, share of the week's events |
| `one_sided_civilian_fatalities` | panel 3, lollipops for non-zero weeks |

Weeks without events are stored as `0`, not omitted.

### Steps

1. **Download the raw GED** (v26.1) into `data/raw/GEDEvent_v26_1.csv`, kept unmodified.
2. **`01_clean_events.py`** validates required columns, unique event ids, parseable dates and `date_start <= date_end`, restricts the data to Sudan and 2023-04-15 to 2025-12-31, derives `combatant_fatalities`, `civilian_fatalities` and `one_sided_civilian_fatalities`, and flags (does not drop) events with invalid coordinates. Output: `data/processed/events_clean.parquet`, one row per event.
3. **`02_build_actor_events.py`** expands each event into actor × event rows (a combat event becomes two rows, one per side; a one-sided event becomes one row, the perpetrator). Output: `data/processed/actor_events.parquet` and `actor_lookup.csv`.
4. **`03_build_weekly_metrics.py`** assigns each event to a Monday-starting week by `date_start`, builds a complete weekly calendar with zero-filled gaps, and derives `one_sided_event_share = one_sided_event_count / event_count` (0 for empty weeks). Output: `data/derived/weekly_metrics.csv` (3,289 rows: 143 weeks for `__ALL__` plus one series per actor).
5. **`07_validate_outputs.py`** runs 146 checks (all pass) over every processed and derived file. `build_all.py` runs all stages in order, stops at the first failure, and also writes `metadata.json` (country, analysis period, week convention, H3 resolution, window size).
6. **`npm run sync-data`** (in `web/`, run automatically by `npm run dev` and `npm run build`) copies the five derived files unchanged into `web/.generated/data/`.
7. **At startup** `src/data/loaders.ts` fetches and parses the files through `parsers.ts`, which fails loudly on schema drift and keeps `null` (not observed) distinct from `0`. Views read only through `src/data/selectors.ts`.
8. **`Timeline.ts`** gets the series for the selected actor (`getWeeklyForActor`; `__ALL__` is read straight from the file) and draws three time-aligned SVG panels with D3 scales: stacked event composition, one-sided share, and one-sided civilian fatalities. Stacking and hit-testing live in `timelineMath.ts`.
9. **Interaction** is layered on top: hover shows a shared crosshair and tooltip, drag-to-zoom on a brush strip rescales the x domain (local state only), and clicking a week or pressing Enter writes `focusWeek` to the global store, which puts the map into week mode and marks T0 elsewhere. A 'View as table' fallback renders the same rows as HTML.
